READ BEFORE PRINTING

GOOD PRINTS START HERE.

01 / CHECK THE SOURCE

More pixels. More room to print.

Effective DPI = source pixels ÷ finished inches. An 1,800-pixel image printed 6 inches wide gives 300 DPI. Increasing output pixels does not add original detail.

02 / MIRROR IF NEEDED

Some transfers need a different view.

Enable horizontal mirroring when your process requires it. Printer drivers or transfer software may also mirror; test first so you do not mirror twice.

03 / TILE A LARGE DESIGN

A small printer can make a bigger page.

Choose A4 or Letter with an overlap. Print each PDF page at 100%, match the artwork in the overlapping strips, and trim and assemble carefully.

TECHNICAL NOTES

KNOW YOUR
PRINT SPECS.

Get the source, scale, and output right.

Will 300 DPI make a blurry image sharp?

No. Output DPI controls how many pixels we export for your requested size. Effective source DPI reports the original detail available before resampling.

Is the downloaded PNG actually tagged with DPI?

Yes. The export writes a PNG pHYs chunk with pixels per meter corresponding to the chosen DPI. Some print applications ignore that metadata, so verify the final physical size.

Do tiled PDFs preserve print scale?

Each PDF page has the selected physical dimensions, and artwork is positioned at the chosen size with overlap. Print at Actual size / 100%. PDF tiles are rasterized at 150 DPI; compare the 50 mm ruler with a real ruler.

Does this provide product-specific sublimation templates?

No. Dimensions are editable and no product fit is promised. Measure your actual blank and follow the manufacturer’s instructions for bleed, safe area, transfer and heat settings.
